Firms are central actors in the economy, and their behavior reflects the interplay between owners’ objectives and external constraints, notably financing frictions. Financial markets are subject to information frictions, transaction costs, imperfect competition and so on, capital suppliers are therefore not passive conduits; their behavior materially shapes firms’ financing conditions and investment outcomes. Yet financing constraints alone do not determine corporate activity. Investment decisions are, without surprise, also driven by the demand side, particularly the nature of firms’ production technologies. Over the past several decades, the widespread adoption of information technologies and the growing role of innovation as a driver of productivity have significantly transformed how firms produce and compete. Understanding these shifts in production technology is essential for explaining firm-level behavior and, ultimately, aggregate economic growth.
